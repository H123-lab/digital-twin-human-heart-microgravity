As our study protocol evolves, I recommend organizing the project into five master datasets rather than one:
File	Purpose	Status
Dataset_01_Screening.xlsx	Screen all ACDC and M&Ms subjects, calculate DTSS, document inclusion/exclusion	Current phase
Dataset_02_Selected_Subjects.xlsx	Complete demographic and imaging metadata for the final 25 participants	Next
Dataset_03_DigitalTwin_Parameters.xlsx	Mesh characteristics, fiber assignment, material properties, boundary conditions, calibrated parameters	Later
Dataset_04_Simulation_Results.xlsx	Earth, Mars, and microgravity outputs (EDV, ESV, EF, wall stress, strain, pressure, etc.)	Results
Dataset_05_Statistical_Analysis.xlsx	Summary statistics, hypothesis tests, confidence intervals, effect sizes, regression, validation metrics	Final analysis

This structure follows good computational cardiovascular research practice and will make your work highly reproducible. It also separates source data, cohort selection, model inputs, simulation outputs, and statistical analyses, which reviewers in journals such as European Heart Journal – Digital Health, npj Digital Medicine, or Computer Methods in Biomechanics and Biomedical Engineering typically expect.

Important Clarification for the Manuscript

This sentence should appear in the Methods:

"No new human participants were recruited. All patient imaging data were obtained from publicly available, previously de-identified cardiac MRI datasets. The study used the ACDC Challenge dataset for digital twin development and the M&Ms Challenge dataset for external validation. Rule-based myocardial fiber architecture was assigned using established anatomical priors rather than patient-specific diffusion tensor MRI."

This statement is technically accurate because neither ACDC nor M&Ms provides diffusion tensor MRI (DT-MRI), so patient-specific myocardial fiber orientation cannot be directly reconstructed from the datasets.
