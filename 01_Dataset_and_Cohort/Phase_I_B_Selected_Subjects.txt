Phase I-B — Definition of Exact Inclusion Criteria and Selection Strategy for the 25 Personalized Cardiac Digital Twins
Objective of Phase I-B
The aim is not to maximize sample size. The aim is to create a high-fidelity, reproducible cohort of patient-specific cardiac digital twins where every selected subject can pass through the complete pipeline:
\text{CMR} \rightarrow \text{Segmentation} \rightarrow 3D Mesh} \rightarrow \text{Fiber Architecture} \rightarrow \text{Physics Calibration} \rightarrow \text{Digital Twin} \rightarrow \text{Gravity Simulation}
For this reason, we will prioritize data completeness and physiological interpretability over disease diversity.
ACDC is particularly suitable because it provides cine CMR, expert ventricular/myocardial annotations, diagnosis categories, and patient metadata including age, height, weight, and cardiac phase information. 
M&Ms is suitable for external validation because it was specifically designed to test generalization across centers, vendors, and diseases, containing 375 heterogeneous CMR datasets from multiple hospitals and scanner vendors.

2. Inclusion Criteria
Core Eligibility Criteria (Mandatory)
A subject will be included only if ALL criteria are satisfied.

A. Imaging Requirements
Required:
✅ Cine cardiac MRI available
✅ Short-axis stack available
✅ Complete end-diastolic (ED) phase
✅ Complete end-systolic (ES) phase
✅ Full ventricular coverage
✅ Expert segmentation available
✅ Image quality acceptable for 3D reconstruction
Rationale:
Digital twin construction requires reliable ventricular geometry. Current MRI-based digital twin pipelines typically begin with segmentation, contour extraction, volumetric mesh generation, and fiber assignment. 

B. Age Criteria
Include:
18–65 years
Reason:
This avoids:
	• pediatric cardiac anatomy variation 
	• extreme age-related remodeling 

C. Anthropometric Criteria
Required:
Variable	Requirement
Height	Available
Weight	Available
BMI	Calculable
Reason:
Body size influences:
	• ventricular dimensions 
	• wall stress 
	• scaling of computational models 

D. Cardiac Function Criteria
Available:
	• LV end-diastolic volume 
	• LV end-systolic volume 
	• LV ejection fraction 
	• Stroke volume

E. Rhythm Criteria
Preferred:
Sinus rhythm
Exclude:
	• atrial fibrillation 
	• paced rhythm 
	• major arrhythmia 
Reason:
The first-generation model focuses on mechanical adaptation to gravity, not electrophysiological remodeling.

3. Disease Distribution Strategy
We should NOT randomly select 15 ACDC patients.
A digital twin paper should demonstrate biological variability.
Recommended ACDC development cohort:
Group	Number
Healthy controls	5
Dilated cardiomyopathy	3
Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy	3
Previous myocardial infarction	3
Abnormal RV	1
Total	15
ACDC contains balanced groups including healthy subjects, myocardial infarction, dilated cardiomyopathy, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, and abnormal RV cases. 

4. Exclusion Criteria
Imaging Exclusions
Remove:
❌ Severe motion artifact
❌ Missing ED frame
❌ Missing ES frame
❌ Incomplete ventricular coverage
❌ Poor segmentation boundaries
❌ Non-diagnostic cine quality

Clinical Exclusions
Remove:
❌ Congenital heart disease
Reason:
Congenital anatomy requires a separate modeling framework.

❌ Severe combined cardiac pathology
Example:
	• infarction + severe dilation 
	• complex valve disease 
Reason:
Too many unknown physiological variables.

❌ Extreme ventricular remodeling
Example:
	• giant aneurysms 
	• massive hypertrophy 
Reason:
May introduce unstable mesh deformation.
